Migration and Distribution of the Tandara Family

Introduction

This chapter follows the movement of individual Tandara family branches after they had taken shape in their original area. Rather than repeating the history of the surname itself, the emphasis is on migration routes, the periods in which those movements can be traced, and the places where new family centres developed.

Ričice remains the starting point of this overview, while every later location is treated as part of a migration route that should be connected with a specific family branch and the available evidence.

Ričice – the point of origin

The earliest reliably known centre of the family is the village of Ričice in the Imotski region. It is here, during the first half of the eighteenth century, that the development of the family can be traced, from which later branches emerged.

For centuries Ričice represented more than just a place of residence. It served as the starting point from which individual members gradually moved to surrounding areas while maintaining an awareness of their shared ancestry.

Ričice through the seasons and from different perspectives

Expansion within the Imotski region

During the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the number of family members steadily increased. As new households formed, some families moved to nearby settlements in search of arable land, opportunities to establish their own homes or better economic conditions.

Such migrations were common among families throughout the Imotski region and represented a natural continuation of demographic development.

Expansion toward Zavelim, Tomislavgrad and Livno

The first documented expansion of the Tandara surname beyond Ričice is linked to the Zavelim region, a wide area encompassing around ten villages. Among them is the village of Tandara, named after the earliest settlers from Ričice – members of the Tandara family line.

Between 1806 and 1819, the first houses were built in this area, followed by the gradual relocation of two families from Ričice. According to family tradition and current research, these were the families of Ante Antin, the founder of the Lovrina branch and later the Križan branch, and of Martin Petrov, the founder of the Tomina branch and the Tromić lineage. Their arrival marked the beginning of the Zavelim branch of the Tandara family, a direct continuation of the original Ričice line.

The Zavelim branch played a key role in preserving the surname, family identity, and the enduring connection to its Ričice roots. With the settlement of these families, a new core of the family was formed, from which additional lines later developed.

The second expansion of the Tandara family most likely moved toward the Tomislavgrad area. From there, some families later relocated to Livno, where their descendants still live today. The Livno branch will be presented in a dedicated chapter titled Livanjske Tandare.

Emigration to Europe

The second half of the twentieth century brought a new wave of migration. Economic circumstances led many families to leave for temporary or permanent work in Western European countries.

The largest number of emigrants settled in:

  • Germany
  • Austria
  • Switzerland
  • United Kingdom

Although they built new homes abroad, most maintained strong ties with their native region, regularly visiting Ričice and preserving family connections.

Overseas migration

During the twentieth century some members of the family emigrated to overseas countries. The most common destinations were:

  • United States
  • Canada
  • Australia

In their new environments, families continued to preserve Croatian identity, traditions and awareness of their origins.

Distribution of the Tandara Family Through Time

The story of the Tandara family in Ričice begins on a very small scale. In 1725, the first known Tandara, or Tandarić, family recorded in Netrub consisted of only three members. From that small family nucleus, over the following three centuries, a family line developed whose descendants gradually spread from Ričice to other parts of Croatia, neighbouring regions and, eventually, countries around the world.

By 1806, Ričice was home to three Tandara families with a total of 20 members. About fifteen years later, four families with 27 members were recorded in Ričice and Zavelim. The growing number of descendants was accompanied by gradual movement to new places, most often in search of land, work and better living conditions.

A particularly valuable snapshot comes from 1948. At that time, Croatia recorded 21 Tandara families with 107 members, living in nine locations. The largest concentration was still in Ričice — 12 families and 69 people — while other families had already settled in Zagreb, Split, Kutjevo, Podravska Slatina, the Bjelovar area, the Đakovo area and elsewhere.

Sixty years later, the picture had changed considerably. According to the data for 2008, Croatia recorded 104 Tandara families with a total of 317 members, distributed across 32 locations. The figures show how the former concentration of the family in its home region developed during the twentieth century into a much broader distribution across Croatia.

According to the data currently available to the project for 2026, Croatia alone has more than 105 Tandara families with more than 330 members. At the same time, it is estimated that approximately the same number of Tandaras now live outside Croatia as within Croatia. This estimate includes descendants and family branches dispersed across several countries and should be understood as a working estimate, since equally complete and comparable official data are not available for every country.

These figures describe more than demographic growth. They reveal the migration path of a family line across three centuries: from one three-member household in Netrub, through gradual expansion within Croatia, to present-day generations living in different countries and on different continents. Each new location on that map represents another family story, another branch and a continuation of the same shared root.

From three members of a single family in 1725 to several hundred descendants three centuries later — this continuity and geographical spread show how profoundly migration has shaped the Tandara family as it exists today.

How migration evidence is verified

The appearance of the same surname in a new place is not by itself sufficient to prove that a person belongs to a particular branch. More reliable connections are based on family relationships, the sequence of generations, places of birth and marriage, family tradition and other available records.

For that reason, the map and accompanying text distinguish documented migration routes from directions that still remain research hypotheses.

Connections between dispersed branches today

Modern communication makes it possible for descendants of distant branches to reconnect, exchange photographs and documents, and compare family information. Such contacts can help fill gaps that older archival sources from a single location cannot resolve on their own.

Conclusion

The migrations of the Tandara family can be viewed as a series of connected routes beginning in Ričice and extending toward neighbouring areas, other parts of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Western Europe and overseas countries.

This page is therefore not a second genealogy. It is the geographical layer of the family history, showing where individual branches moved and which connections still require confirmation through additional documentation.
