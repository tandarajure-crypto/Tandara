THE FOURTH BRANCH OF THE FIRST TANDARA FAMILY

4.0. Mate Tandara – son of Ivan (★ c. 1750 – ✝ D.U.)

Mate Tandara was married to Filipa née Kopač. They had five children: Ivan, Matija, Manda, Jaka and Cecilija; their birth years are unknown. The Tandara surname continued through Ivan.

Biographical note

An interesting oral tradition concerning the origin of this nickname has been preserved among members of this branch of the Tandara family. According to the tradition, some of Mate's descendants performed agricultural work for a family of the Lažeta line. When members of the household and their neighbours asked them, “What was the food like at the Lažeta household?”, they replied: “Like at the cesar's table!”

Whether the word they used was cesar or sesar remains unclear. Although both forms can be found on older gravestones and in oral tradition, most descendants of this family branch are now known as Cesarovići.

Biographical note

In the early 1990s, he joined the defence of Croatia against the aggression of the former Yugoslav People's Army and Serbian paramilitary forces.

Ante served in the Croatian War of Independence from August 1991 as a member of the 105th Brigade of the Croatian Army. In September of the same year, Ante and nineteen other Croatian defenders, most of them from Bjelovar, were captured in Kusonje near Pakrac, tortured and executed. Their remains were exhumed from the Rakov Potok mass grave in January 1992.

Biographical note

Dinko is a volunteer veteran of the Croatian War of Independence. Together with his brother Ante, he joined the defence of Croatia from the beginning of the war as a member of the Bjelovar-Bilogora Police Special Unit “Omega”. During the war he served on several fronts across Croatia, including Pakrac, Bilogora, Maslenica and Velebit.

During the war he lost his older brother Ante, who was killed at Kusonje in September 1991. Dinko continued his wartime and post-war service with a strong focus on Croatian veterans and their families.

At the Ministry of Croatian Veterans he served for four years as an assistant minister under Tomo Medved and was later appointed Director of the Directorate for Advisory, Psychosocial and Health Assistance. He has also had a long association with taekwondo as a national-team competitor, multiple national champion, and founder and coach of the Bjelovar Omega Taekwondo Club.

Biographical note

Marijan Tandara was born in Lumbarda and studied in Sinj, Split and Zagreb. He graduated in medical biochemistry from the Faculty of Pharmacy and Biochemistry, with a thesis on the effects of freezing on human semen. He gained his first professional experience in the IVF laboratory at the Clinic for Women’s Diseases and Obstetrics at Petrova Hospital in Zagreb, where he learned from leading specialists. He earned a master’s degree with research on the biochemical analysis of seminal fluid and IVF outcomes, and a PhD with research on glucose metabolism and the structure of human preimplantation embryos.

At the invitation of Dr Ivan Vlastelić and Prof Ljiljana Randić, he helped establish an IVF laboratory at University Hospital Centre Rijeka. He then moved to University Hospital Centre Split, where he launched and organized the IVF laboratory and introduced medically assisted reproduction (MAR), ICSI, PICSI and TESA procedures; cryopreservation and thawing of gametes and embryos; and new andrology tests, including DNA fragmentation and the HBA test for assessing male fertility. He has written professional and scientific papers in this field and delivered many talks at national and international conferences.

Alongside his hospital work, he teaches at the University of Split School of Medicine, holding the title of assistant professor in the Department of Gynecology and Obstetrics. He is also part of Polyclinic “Šparac”.
