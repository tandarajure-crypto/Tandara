Tandara – Family Genealogy

Introduction

This page does not focus on explaining the origin of the surname, but on relationships between people, generations and family branches. Its purpose is to make it clear who belongs to whom, how each line continues, and where the available documentation comes to an end.

The historical background of the surname is presented in the Origin section, while the movement of individual branches is covered on the Migration page. Genealogy therefore remains the central place for the structure of the family and verified family relationships.

How to read the genealogy

The family trees are read from older to younger generations. Each person is placed within the appropriate family line, and the links between parents, children and spouses make it possible to follow the branching of the family without repeating general historical descriptions.

Text accompanying the tree adds information only where a diagram needs explanation or context. This keeps the genealogy clear, while historical, migration and other background material remains on the pages specifically devoted to those subjects.

Reliability and limits of the sources

The reliability of the information is not identical for every period. Fewer direct records survive for the earliest generations, while parish registers and later documents allow people and family relationships to be connected with much greater certainty.

When different sources do not agree, or when direct confirmation is missing, the information is not treated in the same way as a fully documented fact. Such points remain open for further verification and future additions.

Development of Family Branches

As the number of family members increased, individual households began to form their own separate units. From a single original family, distinct branches gradually emerged, remaining connected through shared ancestry.

Although they lived in different parts of the village or later in other places, the awareness of belonging to the same lineage was preserved across many generations.

Family and Everyday Life

Throughout most of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, members of the family lived primarily from agriculture and livestock farming, which was typical for the population of the Imotski region. Family households often included several generations under the same roof. This way of life helped preserve common property, ensured mutual support and enabled the transfer of experience between older and younger family members.

Family Trees

The central part of this genealogy consists of family trees. They provide an overview of all known generations and clearly show the connections between individual branches of the lineage.

For each person, whenever information was available, the following details are listed:

  • year of birth,
  • year of death,
  • name of spouse,
  • children,
  • place of residence.

This makes it possible to easily follow the development of the family over time.

Public and Protected Genealogical Records

The public genealogical display contains only information that may be published under the archive rules. Protected information is kept separately and does not change a person’s position in the family tree; it changes only the amount of information visible to the public. Detailed publication and protection rules are set out in the Privacy Policy.

Requests for corrections, additions or access to protected information are submitted through Contact & Collaboration and are reviewed individually.

Photographs and Family Heritage

Photographs, old family documents and other historical materials give special value to the genealogy. Such material complements written sources and helps preserve not only names and dates but also fragments of everyday life of earlier generations.

Each new photograph or document represents a valuable contribution to the further development of this project.

Corrections and additions

Genealogical information is changed only when a new document, photograph or reliable family record provides information that can be linked to an existing person or branch. A new claim does not automatically replace an existing entry; it is first compared with the sources already collected.

This approach allows each addition to remain part of the same genealogical structure while errors can be corrected without losing previously established relationships.

Conclusion

The value of this genealogy lies not in repeating historical descriptions, but in the clarity of the family relationships it presents. Its task is to show continuity between generations, the branching of family lines and the points at which documentation is still incomplete.

Genealogy therefore serves as the central map of the family, while historical, geographical and linguistic explanations remain in separate thematic sections.
