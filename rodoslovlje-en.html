Tandara Family Genealogy

Introduction

This page focuses not on explaining the origin of the surname, but on relationships between people, generations and family branches. Its purpose is to make it clear from the genealogical presentation who belongs to whom, how each family line continues and where the available documentation ends.

The historical background of the surname is covered in the Origin section, while the migration of individual branches is presented on the Migration page. Genealogy therefore remains the central place for the family structure and verified relationships.

How to Read the Genealogy

Family trees are read from older to younger generations. Each person is placed within his or her family line, while the relationships between parents, children and spouses make it possible to follow the branching of the family without returning to general historical descriptions.

The accompanying text supplements the graphical presentation only where additional information or explanation is required. In this way the genealogy remains clear, while historical, migration and other contexts remain on the pages to which they belong.

Research Sources and Bibliography

The genealogy of the Tandara family is based on archival and published sources listed in the bibliography accompanying the research material. For clarity, the sources are divided into unpublished and published material.

A particularly important place in this research is held by the works of Milan Lažeta, Rodoslovlje plemena Tandara and Ričice u nama, as well as the publication by Viktor Vito Grabovac Ričice kroz vijekove. These valuable works laid the foundation for further study of the history and origins of our family and remain among the most important sources for understanding the heritage of the Tandara surname and family.

Reliability and Limitations of the Sources

The reliability of the information is not the same for every historical period. Fewer direct records have survived for the earliest generations, while parish registers and later documents allow people and family relationships to be connected with considerably greater certainty.

When different sources do not agree, or when direct confirmation is missing, the information is not treated in the same way as a fully documented fact. Such cases remain open for further verification and future additions.

Development of the Family Branches

As the number of family members increased, individual households gradually developed into separate family units. From one original family, different branches emerged while remaining connected through their common origin.

Although they lived in different parts of the village, and later in different places altogether, awareness of belonging to the same family was preserved through many generations.

Family and Everyday Life

During most of the 18th and 19th centuries, members of the family lived primarily from agriculture and livestock farming, as was common among the population of the Imotski region. Family households often included several generations living under the same roof. This way of life contributed to the preservation of family relationships, customs and oral tradition.

Family Trees

Family trees form the central part of this genealogy. They provide an overview of all known generations and clearly show the connections between the individual branches of the family.

For each person, where the information was available, the following details are provided:

  • year of birth,
  • year of death,
  • name of spouse,
  • children,
  • place of residence.

In this way, the development of the family through time can be followed easily.

Photographs and Family Heritage

Photographs, old family documents and other historical material give genealogy particular value. Such materials supplement written sources and help preserve not only names and dates, but also traces of the everyday lives of earlier generations.

Every new photograph or document represents a valuable contribution to the further development of this project.

Corrections and Additions

Genealogical information is changed only when a new document, photograph or reliable family information provides data that can be connected with an existing person or family branch. A new claim does not automatically replace an existing record; it is first compared with the sources already collected.

In this way, every addition remains part of the same genealogical structure, while possible errors can be corrected without losing previously established relationships.

Conclusion

The value of this genealogy does not lie in the quantity of repeated historical descriptions, but in the clarity of family relationships. Its purpose is to show the continuity of generations, the branching of the family and the places where the documentation is still incomplete.

The genealogy therefore serves as the central map of the family, while historical, geographical and linguistic explanations remain in separate thematic sections.
