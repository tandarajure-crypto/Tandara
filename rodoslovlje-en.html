Tandara Family Genealogy

Introduction

This page focuses not on explaining the origin of the surname, but on the relationships between individuals, generations, and family branches. Its purpose is to make the genealogical structure clear: who belongs to whom, how each line continues, and where the available documentation comes to an end.

The historical background of the surname is covered in the Origin section, while the movements of individual branches are presented on the Migrations page. The genealogy therefore remains the central place for the family structure and verified kinship links.

How to Read the Genealogy

The family trees are read from older to younger generations. Each person appears within his or her own family line, while the links between parents, children, and spouses make it possible to follow the branching of the family without returning to broader historical descriptions.

The text accompanying the tree supplements the visual display only where an additional detail or explanation is needed. This keeps the genealogy easy to follow, while historical, migration-related, and other contextual information remains on the pages where it belongs.

Names for Generations of Ancestors

In English, the closest generations of ancestors have commonly used relationship names, such as father, grandfather, great-grandfather, and great-great-grandfather. For more distant generations, English does not have distinct traditional names corresponding to the terms preserved in Croatian family and folk tradition. For that reason, from the fifth ancestral generation onward, this genealogy retains the traditional Croatian names used by the Tandara family.

Research Sources and Bibliography

The genealogy of the Tandara family is based on archival and published sources listed in the bibliography accompanying the research material. For clarity, the sources are divided into unpublished and published materials.

A particularly important place in this research is held by Milan Lažeta's works Rodoslovlje plemena Tandara and Ričice u nama, as well as Viktor Vito Grabovac's publication Ričice kroz vijekove. These valuable works laid the foundations for further study of the history and origins of our family and remain among the most important sources for understanding the heritage of the Tandara surname and family.

Reliability and Limitations of the Sources

The reliability of the information is not the same for all periods. Fewer direct records survive for the oldest generations, while parish registers and later documents allow individuals and family relationships to be linked with considerably greater certainty.

When different sources do not agree, or when direct confirmation is lacking, the information is not treated in the same way as a fully documented fact. Such points remain open to further verification and future additions.

Development of the Family Branches

As the number of family members increased, individual households gradually formed distinct family units. From one original family, different branches developed while remaining connected by their common ancestry.

Although they lived in different parts of the village and later in other places, awareness of belonging to the same family was preserved through many generations.

Family and Everyday Life

For much of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, members of the family made their living primarily from agriculture and livestock farming, as was common among the population of the Imotski region. Family households often included several generations living under one roof. This way of life helped preserve family ties, customs, and oral tradition.

Family Trees

The family trees form the central part of this genealogy. They provide an overview of all known generations and clearly show how the individual branches of the family are connected.

For each person, where the information was available, the following details are given:

  • year of birth,
  • year of death,
  • name of spouse,
  • children,
  • place of residence.

This makes it possible to follow the development of the family through time with ease.

Photographs and Family Heritage

Photographs, old family documents, and other historical materials add particular value to the genealogy. They complement the written sources and help preserve not only names and dates, but also traces of everyday life in earlier generations.

Every new photograph or document is a valuable contribution to the continued development of this project.

Corrections and Additions

Genealogical information is changed only when a new document, photograph, or reliable family account provides information that can be linked to an existing person or branch. A new claim does not automatically replace the existing record; it is first compared with the sources already collected.

In this way, each addition remains part of the same genealogical whole, while possible errors can be corrected without losing previously established relationships.

Conclusion

The value of this genealogy lies not in the amount of repeated historical description, but in the clarity of the family relationships. Its purpose is to show the continuity of generations, the branching of the family, and the points at which the documentation is still incomplete.

The genealogy therefore serves as the central map of the family, while historical, geographical, and linguistic explanations remain in separate thematic sections.
